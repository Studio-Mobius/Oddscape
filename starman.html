Reach for the Stars
Beyond Earth Terrain Study

Lunar Landscapes, Modelled as Terrain

Oddscape-style terrain work does not have to stop at Earth. NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter has mapped the Moon in extraordinary detail, including laser-altimeter elevation data through LOLA — the lunar equivalent of LiDAR.

These datasets make it possible to study lunar valleys, rilles, craters, mountain fronts and landing sites as physical landscapes rather than distant abstractions.

Apollo 15

Hadley Rille and the Apennine mountain front. A dramatic lunar landscape with the strongest Oddscape feel for a first study.

Apollo 17

The Taurus–Littrow Valley, the final Apollo landing site, framed by steep massifs and complex lunar terrain.

Apollo 11

The Sea of Tranquillity. Less dramatic topographically, but instantly recognisable as the first human landing site on another world.

Data Sources

NASA’s LRO and LOLA datasets provide lunar elevation and terrain information for exploration, landing-site assessment and surface interpretation.

First Proposed Study

The first Oddscape lunar terrain study will focus on Apollo 15, Hadley Rille and the Apennine Front — a place where mountain, valley and sinuous rille combine into one of the most visually powerful explored landscapes on the Moon.
