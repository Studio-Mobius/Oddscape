Snowdon & Crib Goch
Snowdon & Crib Goch Terrain Study. Hillshade terrain visualisation generated from Welsh Government 1m LiDAR DTM data.
A high-resolution terrain study of the Snowdon massif, centred on Crib Goch, Llyn Llydaw, Glaslyn and the upper slopes of Yr Wyddfa.

Llyn Llydaw and the Snowdon Massif

The landscape is one of Britain's most dramatic mountain environments. Glacial erosion has carved steep ridges, cirques and lake basins into the volcanic bedrock, creating a terrain that remains legible at every scale, from the broad mountain profile to the narrow knife-edge of Crib Goch itself.

Bare-earth LiDAR data reveals the underlying structure of the landscape beneath vegetation and modern infrastructure. Seen from above, the relationship between ridge, valley and water becomes immediately apparent.

Llyn Llydaw forms a visual centre to the study area, while Crib Goch traces a sharp arc across the mountain system, separating neighbouring glacial landforms.

Landscape Character

The Snowdon massif is defined by steep volcanic terrain shaped by successive glaciations. Ridges, corries, tarns and hanging valleys combine to create one of the most recognisable mountain landscapes in Wales.

Terrain Source

Generated from Welsh Government 1m LiDAR DTM data. The digital terrain model represents the underlying landform, removing vegetation and surface clutter to reveal the bare-earth structure of the landscape.

Visual Interpretation

Hillshade, terrain rendering and elevation modelling highlight the physical relationships between mountain ridges, glacial basins and water bodies.

Applications

Suitable for landscape interpretation, heritage projects, education, environmental studies, visitor engagement, terrain visualisation and digital landscape presentation.

Terrain Outputs

The terrain model allows the massif to be explored as a continuous sculptural landscape. Features often experienced individually on the ground can instead be understood as part of a larger geological and topographic whole.

Technical Summary

This terrain study covers the central Snowdon massif, including Crib Goch, Llyn Llydaw, Glaslyn and surrounding upland landforms. Generated from 1m resolution LiDAR terrain data, the model captures the geological structure and glacial morphology of one of Wales' most celebrated mountain landscapes.

The study demonstrates how high-resolution terrain visualisation can reveal patterns and relationships that are often difficult to appreciate from ground level alone. Ridges, lake basins, escarpments and mountain slopes emerge as connected elements within a single landscape system.
