A brief history of exploring Mars

From observation to exploration

Mars has moved from a point of light, to a photographed world, to terrain crossed by robotic explorers. Each mission has revealed another layer of the planet's history.

1965 · The first close look

Mariner 4 changes everything

Mariner 4 became the first spacecraft to fly past Mars and return close-range photographs of another planet. Its twenty-two images revealed an ancient cratered landscape. For the first time, Mars was no longer imagined — it had been seen.

Explore Mariner 4 at NASA Science

1971 · Mapping an entire planet

Mariner 9 enters orbit

Mariner 9 became the first spacecraft to orbit another planet. As a global dust storm cleared, the mission revealed immense shield volcanoes, dried river channels and the canyon system now known as Valles Marineris.

Explore Mariner 9 at NASA Science

1976 · Humanity lands on Mars

Viking begins surface exploration

NASA's Viking landers returned the first sustained observations from the Martian surface. Their cameras, weather instruments and scientific experiments changed Mars from a remote astronomical body into a place that could be examined directly.

Explore Viking 1 at NASA Science

1997–Present · Crossing the landscape

The age of the rovers

Pathfinder, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity and Perseverance transformed Mars into terrain that could be crossed, photographed and investigated metre by metre. Their discoveries have revealed ancient lakes, river deltas and environments shaped by water and climate change.

Explore Mars missions at NASA Science

Introducing OddMars™

Mars, reconstructed from measurement rather than imagination

OddMars™ is reconstructed from NASA Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter measurements. Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris, Hellas Planitia and thousands of impact structures are represented as measurable terrain rather than an artist's impression.

Global terrain

Explore Mars as a complete landscape and discover how volcanoes, canyon systems, impact basins, northern lowlands and ancient highlands relate across an entire world.

OddView™

Rotate, zoom and inspect the Martian surface in real time, or place the planet into a room, garden, classroom or gallery through augmented reality.

PlanetOdd™

Future releases will add named destinations, geological provinces, mission landing sites and increasingly detailed planetary terrain across the Solar System.
