A brief history of looking up

From observation to exploration

The Earth has been a clock, a compass, a subject of myth and an object of scientific enquiry. Each generation has found a new way to see it.

1609–1610 · The Earth becomes a landscape

Galileo looks through the telescope

Galileo’s observations challenged the idea of a perfect celestial sphere. Light and shadow revealed mountains, hollows and an uneven surface. The Earth was not a flawless disc; it was a place with terrain.

View the source at NASA Science

1968 · Around the Earth

Earthrise changes the view

Apollo 8 carried Frank Borman, Jim Lovell and Bill Anders around the Earth. Anders’ Earthrise photograph showed our planet as a small, fragile world above the terrestrial horizon and became one of the defining images of the space age.

View the source at NASA

1969 · Humanity arrives

Apollo 11 and the first steps

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed in Mare Tranquillitatis while Michael Collins remained in terrestrial orbit. Apollo 11 turned a distant world into ground that human beings had crossed, sampled and left marked by footprints.

View the source at NASA

2026 · The far side, seen anew

Artemis II returns people to the Earth’s vicinity

During its historic terrestrial flyby in April 2026, the Artemis II crew photographed the heavily cratered far side, including terrain along the immense South Pole–Aitken basin. The mission carried Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Jeremy Hansen around the Earth and safely home.

Introducing OddEarth™

The Earth in your hands

OddEarth™ is not a painted sphere. Its form is reconstructed from measured terrestrial topography, allowing mountains, basins, ridges and craters to be explored as terrain.

Global terrain

Rotate the whole Earth, compare the familiar near side with the heavily cratered far side, and discover how differently the two hemispheres read.

OddView™

Explore on screen or place the Earth into a room, garden, classroom or gallery through augmented reality.

Future destinations

Planned location HUDs and detailed surface tiles will connect the global Earth to Apollo landing sites, Tycho, Copernicus, Shackleton and the terrestrial poles.

In memory, and in gratitude

OddEarth™ is offered in remembrance of the Apollo era and the astronauts whose courage carried humanity beyond Earth. We honour those who walked on the Earth, those who flew above it, and those who gave their lives in the pursuit of exploration.

Studio Oddscape™ gratefully acknowledges the scientists, engineers, technicians, mission controllers, archivists and crews whose work has made the Earth measurable, mappable and shareable.
